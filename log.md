What is a Log Regression Channel?

A log regression channel is a trend channel drawn around a logarithmic regression curve rather than a straight line.

Instead of measuring price on a normal (linear) scale:

Linear scale treats a move from £10 to £20 the same as £100 to £110.
Logarithmic scale treats price moves in percentage terms.

This is important because financial markets typically compound over time. A stock increasing from £10 to £20 is a 100% gain, 
while £100 to £110 is only a 10% gain.

The channel consists of:

Regression Line

The statistical best fit line through historical prices on a logarithmic scale.

Upper Channel

Represents an area where price becomes historically overextended.

Lower Channel

Represents an area where price becomes historically undervalued.

How is it Used?
1. Identify Long Term Trends

The regression line shows the average growth path of the asset.

Price above the regression line = stronger than average growth.
Price below the regression line = weaker than average growth.

Example:

The Nasdaq has historically spent decades rising inside broad logarithmic channels.
Bitcoin analysts frequently use log regression channels to estimate long term trend ranges.
2. Find Potential Buying Zones

Many traders look for:

Lower channel touches as accumulation zones.
Upper channel touches as profit taking zones.

For example:

Position	InterpretationLower Band	Historically cheap
Middle Line	Fair value trend
Upper Band	Historically expensive
3. Identify Market Extremes

When price reaches the upper channel:

Market may be becoming euphoric.
Risk/reward becomes less attractive.

When price reaches the lower channel:

Fear may be excessive.
Long term value may be improving.

This does not mean price will reverse immediately, but it highlights statistical extremes.

4. Estimate Future Price Paths

Because the channel is based on trend statistics, traders can project the channel forward.

This helps answer questions such as:

Is the current rally unusually strong?
Is the market moving within historical norms?
Has a potential bubble formed?
Why Use a Log Regression Channel Instead of a Linear Channel?
Better for Exponential Growth Assets

Many assets grow by percentages rather than fixed amounts.

Example:

Year	Price1	£10
2	£20
3	£40
4	£80

A linear chart makes this look increasingly steep.

A logarithmic chart shows a consistent growth rate.

Reduces Distortion

Long time series often become difficult to analyse on a linear chart because recent price movements dominate the chart.

A logarithmic regression channel:

Normalises percentage growth.
Makes decades of data comparable.
Produces more meaningful support and resistance levels.
Advantages

✅ Works well for long term investing

✅ Useful for stocks, indices, and cryptocurrencies

✅ Helps identify statistical overvaluation and undervaluation

✅ Handles exponential growth better than linear trend lines

✅ Allows forward trend projections

Limitations

❌ Not useful for short term trading

❌ Assumes historical growth characteristics continue

❌ Can break down during regime changes

❌ A touch of the channel does not guarantee a reversal

❌ Different lookback periods can produce different channels

Practical Example: Bitcoin

Bitcoin is one of the most common uses of a logarithmic regression channel.

Historically:

Bear market lows have often occurred near the lower channel.
Bull market peaks have often occurred near the upper channel.
The centre line has acted as a long term fair value estimate.

This helps investors judge whether Bitcoin is:

Historically cheap,
Fairly valued,
Historically expensive,

relative to its long term adoption trend.

When Would You Use It?

A log regression channel is most useful when:

Analysing assets with long price histories.
Looking at multi year or multi decade trends.
Evaluating whether an asset is statistically overextended.
Estimating long term support and resistance.
Studying assets that compound or grow exponentially.

For short term trading over days or weeks, indicators such as moving averages, Bollinger Bands, VWAP, or trend channels are usually more useful. For long term investors, however, a logarithmic regression channel can provide a powerful framework for understanding where price sits relative to its historical growth trend.
