## Ichimoku Cloud (Ichimoku Kinko Hyo)

Ichimoku Kinko Hyo is a complete trading framework developed by Japanese journalist Goichi Hosoda in the late 1930s and published in the 1960s.

Unlike indicators such as MACD or moving averages that focus on a single aspect of the market, Ichimoku attempts to show five things simultaneously:

Trend direction
Trend strength
Momentum
Support and resistance
Potential future price levels

The name roughly translates to:

"One glance equilibrium chart"

The goal is that a trader should be able to look at the chart and quickly determine whether the market is bullish, bearish, or neutral.

### The Five Components
1. **Tenkan-sen (Conversion Line)**

Often called the fast line.

Calculation:

$$
\text{Conversion Line} = \frac{\text{Highest High}(9) + \text{Lowest Low}(9)}{2}
$$

This is not a moving average.

It is the midpoint of the previous 9 periods.

Purpose

Measures short-term momentum.

Example

Last 9 days:

Highest price = £120
Lowest price = £100

Tenkan-sen:

$$
\text{Conversion Line} = \frac{120 + 100}{2} = 110
$$

If price is above 110, short-term momentum is bullish.

2. **Kijun-sen (Base Line)**

Often called the slow line.

Calculation:

$$
\text{Base Line} = \frac{\text{Highest High}(26) + \text{Lowest Low}(26)}{2}
$$

Purpose

Represents the medium-term trend.

Example

Last 26 days:

Highest = £130
Lowest = £90

Kijun-sen:

$$
\text{Base Line} = \frac{130 + 90}{2} = 110
$$

Many traders use the Kijun-sen similarly to a moving average.

3. **Senkou Span A (Leading Span A)**

Calculation:

$$
\text{Leading Span A} = \frac{\text{Tenkan} + \text{Kijun}}{2}
$$

Then projected 26 periods into the future.

Example
Tenkan = 112
Kijun = 108

$$
\text{Leading Span A} = \frac{112 + 108}{2} = 110
$$

Plot 110 twenty-six periods ahead.

4. **Senkou Span B (Leading Span B)**

Calculation:

$$
\text{Leading Span B} = \frac{\text{Highest High}(52) + \text{Lowest Low}(52)}{2}
$$

Projected 26 periods into the future.

Example
Highest 52-day price = £150
Lowest 52-day price = £70

$$
\text{Leading Span B} = \frac{150 + 70}{2} = 110
$$

Projected forward 26 periods.

5. **Chikou Span (Lagging Span)**

Current closing price shifted backward 26 periods.

Purpose

Allows comparison between today's price and price action 26 periods ago.

Acts as a confirmation tool.

### The Cloud (Kumo)

The most distinctive part of Ichimoku.

The area between Senkou Span A and Senkou Span B forms the cloud.

- Green Bullish cloud when: Span A > Span B
- Red Bearish environment, Red Cloud when: Span A < Span B

**Cloud Interpretation**
- Price Above Cloud, strong bullish trend.  Price ↑
Market structure favours buyers.

- Price Below Cloud. Strong bearish trend. Price ↓
 Market structure favours sellers.

- Price Inside Cloud. Neutral market. High uncertainty.
Many traders avoid trades while price is inside the cloud.

### Major Trading Signals
1. **Tenkan-Kijun Cross**

**Bullish**
- Tenkan crosses above Kijun.
- Similar to a moving average crossover.
- Comparable to a bullish MACD crossover.

$$
\text{Tenkan}_{t-1} \le \text{Kijun}_{t-1} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Tenkan}_{t} > \text{Kijun}_{t}
$$

**Bearish**

- Tenkan crosses below Kijun.
- Suggests weakening momentum.
- Signal Strength Depends on Location

$$
\text{Tenkan}_{t-1} \ge \text{Kijun}_{t-1} \quad \text{and} \quad \text{Tenkan}_{t} < \text{Kijun}_{t}
$$


**Very Strong Bullish Signal**

- Bullish crossover above the cloud.
- All elements agree.

**Moderate Signal**

- Bullish crossover inside cloud.
- Less reliable.

**Weak Signal**

- Bullish crossover below cloud.
- Often just a counter-trend rally.


**Cloud Breakout**

- One of the most popular Ichimoku strategies.
- Bullish Breakout
- Price moves: Below Cloud ↓ Inside Cloud ↓ Above Cloud
- Many trend traders enter after the breakout.

Example:

- A stock trades around £95 beneath the cloud.
- Weeks later:

- Price rises to £110
- Breaks above cloud
- Cloud turns green

This is often viewed as a major trend change.

**Bearish Breakout**

- Price falls through cloud support.
- Indicates a possible new downtrend.

**Chikou Span Confirmation**

Bullish conditions:

- Price above cloud
- Tenkan above Kijun
- Chikou above historical prices

All three align.

*This is considered a high-conviction setup.*

### Complete Bullish Ichimoku Setup

The strongest configuration is:

- Price > Cloud
- Tenkan > Kijun
- Cloud is Green
- Chikou > Historical Price


- This is known by some traders as full bullish alignment.

Example

- Current Price = £125
- Tenkan = £120
- Kijun = £115
- Span A = £118
- Span B = £110
- Chikou above prior price action

- Every component is bullish.
- Trend-following traders will usually favour long positions.

Practical Example: S&P 500:

Stage 1 *Bearish.*

- Market correction.
- Price below cloud
- Red cloud
- Tenkan below Kijun

Stage 2 *Neutral.*
- Recovery begins.
- Price enters cloud

Stage 3 *Bullish.*

- Price breaks above cloud.
- Price above cloud
- Tenkan above Kijun
- Cloud turns green

Stage 4 *Bullish.*

- Future cloud thickens upward.
- Trend gains strength.

- Many position traders remain invested until price falls back below the Kijun or cloud.

### Strengths of Ichimoku
1. Complete Trading System

Unlike MACD, RSI, or moving averages, Ichimoku combines:

- Trend
- Momentum
- Support
- Resistance
- Trade confirmation

A trader often needs fewer additional indicators.

2. Future Support and Resistance

Most indicators only look backward.

Ichimoku projects the cloud 26 periods into the future.
This helps identify likely future support and resistance zones.

Example:

- Current price: 125
- Cloud projected ahead: 120-115 support area
- Useful for trade planning.

3. Excellent in Trending Markets

This is where Ichimoku shines.

Strong trends can be ridden for months.

Examples:

- S&P 500 bull markets
- Major forex trends
- Bitcoin trending phases
- Commodity super-cycles

4. Reduces False Signals

A moving average crossover alone may create many entries.

Ichimoku asks:

Is price above the cloud?
Is the cloud bullish?
Is the crossover occurring in the right location?

That extra filtering can improve signal quality.

5. Dynamic Support and Resistance

The cloud often acts as support during uptrends.

Example:

Price pulls back
Touches top of cloud
Resumes rally


Many traders use this for entries.

### Weaknesses of Ichimoku
1. Complex for Beginners
- Most indicators have one or two lines.

Ichimoku has:

- Five lines
- Future projections
- Multiple confirmation rules

- New traders often find charts overwhelming.

2. Lagging Indicator

- Despite its predictive appearance, Ichimoku is still based entirely on historical prices.
- Major moves can occur before signals fully develop.
- Similar to moving averages, it reacts rather than predicts.

3. Poor in Sideways Markets

- This is arguably its biggest weakness.

Consider a market trading:
100 , 105 , 98 , 103 , 101

- Price repeatedly enters and exits the cloud.
- Signals become unreliable.
- Whipsaws increase significantly.

4. Not Ideal for Very Short Time Frames

- 1-minute charts
- 5-minute charts

- Noise often overwhelms the signals.

Many professional users prefer:

- Daily charts
- Weekly charts
- 4-hour forex charts
- where trends are more meaningful.

5. Can Be Late

A substantial part of a trend may have already occurred by the time:

- Price crosses the cloud
- Tenkan crosses Kijun
- Cloud turns bullish

- You gain reliability at the cost of early entry.

### Comparison with MACD and Golden Cross

| Feature            | Ichimoku  | MACD      | 20/200 Golden Cross |
|--------------------|-----------|-----------|---------------------|
| Trend Direction    | Excellent | Good      | Excellent           |
| Momentum           | Good      | Excellent | Poor                |
| Support/Resistance | Excellent | None      | Limited             |
| Trend Strength     | Excellent | Moderate  | Moderate            |
| Early Signals      | Moderate  | Excellent | Poor                |
| False Signals      | Moderate  | Moderate  | Lower               |
| Complexity         | High      | Medium    | Low                 |

### How Many Professional Trend Traders Use It

A common institutional-style workflow is:

- Use the 200-day moving average to determine the long-term trend.

- Use Ichimoku Cloud to identify trend structure and support/resistance.
Use MACD or price action to time entries.

For example:

- Price above 200-day MA.
- Price above cloud.
- Cloud green.
- Tenkan crosses above Kijun.
- MACD turns positive.

When all five conditions align, traders often regard the market as being in a high-quality bullish trend.

*This illustrates why many traders view Ichimoku as a complete trend framework, MACD as a momentum tool, and the Golden Cross as a long-term trend confirmation signal. Together they provide different pieces of information about the same market rather than* competing with one another.*
